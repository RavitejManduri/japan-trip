# Japan Trip: Must-Visit Places Not in the Probesten Itinerary

Trip: Probesten Tours quote #QT016156, 25 Nov – 3 Dec 2026 (Osaka 4N + Tokyo 4N), honeymoon.
Prepared: 24 Oct 2026.

**Already in Probesten's itinerary:** Fushimi Inari, Kinkaku-ji, Nara Park and Todai-ji, Amanohashidate, Osaka Wonder Cruise, Shibuya Sky, teamLab Planets, Mt Fuji 5th Station with Hakone and Lake Ashi, Tokyo DisneySea.

**Not included:** the Arashiyama bamboo grove. Probesten's Kyoto tour covers only Fushimi Inari, Kinkaku-ji, Nara Park and Todai-ji.

---

## Must visit

### 1. Kiyomizu-dera, Kyoto
**Why it matters:**
- Founded in 778, it's one of Kyoto's oldest temples and part of the UNESCO World Heritage site "Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto".
- Its famous wooden stage stands on 139 pillars about 13 m above the hillside, held together without a single nail.
- In Japanese, "jumping off the stage at Kiyomizu" means taking a big leap of faith.

**Why now:** the hillside below the stage is covered in maples, which turn red in late November. The temple also opens at night for an autumn light-up during this season.

**Don't miss:**
- Otowa waterfall: you drink from one of three streams for long life, love or success.
- Jishu Shrine, a small shrine to love and matchmaking. Check whether it's open, because it has been under restoration.

**Time:** 1.5–2 hours

### 2. Yasaka pagoda, the old streets and Gion, Kyoto
**Why it matters:**
- Yasaka pagoda is a 5-storey, 46 m wooden pagoda rebuilt in 1440. It's the classic "old Kyoto" photo.
- Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka are preserved stone lanes lined with wooden tea houses and shops.
- Gion is Japan's most famous geisha district. On Hanamikoji street you may see a geiko or maiko in the evening. Look, but don't chase them for photos.
- Yasaka Shrine is more than 1,350 years old and home to Gion Matsuri, one of Japan's biggest festivals. Its lanterns glow at night.

**Why now:** it's a natural walk downhill from Kiyomizu, so it adds almost no travel time.

**Time:** 2–3 hours on foot

### 3. Arashiyama bamboo grove + Togetsukyo bridge, Kyoto
**Why it matters:**
- Arashiyama was a country retreat for Kyoto's nobility over 1,000 years ago.
- The bamboo grove's rustling sound is on the Japanese government's list of the "100 Soundscapes of Japan".
- Togetsukyo means "moon-crossing bridge". An emperor named it in the 13th century after watching the moon appear to cross it.
- Next to the grove is Tenryu-ji, a UNESCO-listed Zen temple.

**Why now:** late November is peak colour for the hills behind the bridge.

**Tip:** go early or late in the day, because the bamboo path gets very crowded by mid-morning.

**Time:** 2 hours

### 4. Sagano scenic train + Hozugawa river boat, Kyoto
**Why it matters:**
- The Sagano Romantic Train is a vintage train running 25 minutes along the Hozu river gorge, on old railway track saved for sightseeing.
- The Hozugawa boat is a trip of about 2 hours and 16 km downriver on a wooden boat steered by boatmen with poles and oars. The route dates from 1606, when the river was cleared to carry goods to Kyoto.
- Together they're a half-day loop through the mountains that most tour groups never do.

**Why now:** the gorge is at its most colourful in late November.

**Book:** train tickets go on sale **29 Oct 2026 at 10:00 Japan time (6:30am India time)**, and they sell out. Book the boat online as well.

**Time:** half a day, ending at the bamboo grove (#3), so these two make one full Arashiyama day.

### 5. Eikando autumn night light-up, Kyoto
**Why it matters:**
- Eikando (Zenrin-ji) was founded in 853. Since ancient times it has been nicknamed "Momiji no Eikando", meaning "Eikando of the autumn leaves". It's considered Kyoto's single best temple for autumn colour.
- It has about 3,000 maple trees.
- Its rare "Mikaeri Amida" statue is a Buddha looking back over his shoulder.

**Why now:** the night light-up happens only in autumn, roughly early November to early December. The lit maples reflect in the temple pond. It's one of the most romantic sights of the whole trip.

**Time:** about 1.5 hours, plus a queue on weekends

### 6. Senso-ji, Asakusa, Tokyo
**Why it matters:**
- Tokyo's oldest temple, founded in 628. Legend says two fishermen pulled a statue of Kannon, the goddess of mercy, out of the river here.
- The Kaminarimon ("Thunder Gate") with its giant red lantern is one of Tokyo's best-known landmarks.
- Nakamise street, about 250 m long, has sold snacks and souvenirs to visitors for centuries.
- It's the best place to feel old Tokyo next to the modern city.

**Why now:** go very early, around 7–8am, and you'll have it almost to yourselves before the crowds. Then do the Asakusa street-food crawl (Benitsuru pancakes, Age.3, Hokkaido Milk Bar, Ginkado, Kagetsudo).

**Time:** 1–1.5 hours, plus food

### 7. Chureito Pagoda + Lake Kawaguchi, Mt Fuji
**Why it matters:**
- Chureito Pagoda is a 5-storey pagoda built in 1963 as a peace memorial. With Mt Fuji behind it, it's probably the most photographed view in Japan. You climb 398 steps to the viewpoint.
- Lake Kawaguchi is one of the Fuji Five Lakes, with open views of the mountain across the water.
- Mt Fuji became a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2013 as a sacred mountain and a source of art.

**Why now:**
- Late autumn and early winter give the clearest Fuji views of the year, usually with fresh snow on top.
- The maples along the lake may still have some colour.
- Probesten's Fuji tour goes to Hakone instead, where Fuji is often hidden behind hills or cloud.

**Time:** a full day. It's best with a ryokan night so you see Fuji again at sunrise.

## Good to add if time allows
- **Kasuga Taisha** and the **Nigatsudo view**, Nara: easy to add while you're in Nara.
- **Dotonbori at night**, Osaka: costs no daytime, since it's your first evening.
- **Osaka Castle**: fits into the Osaka pass day (tower entry ~¥1,200, not covered by the pass).
- **Kamakura + Enoshima**: lovely, but it needs a whole extra day.

## Fine to skip
- **Mt Wakakusa**: may already be closed for the season after 23 Nov.
- **Shiraito Falls**: needs a private car (~₹15,500) and half a day.
- **Adashino Nenbutsu-ji**, **Harajuku**, **Akihabara**, **Toyosu rooftop footbath**: nice, but not essential.

## What it takes
- Fitting all 7 must-visits in needs **at least 2 extra days**: one more day in Kyoto and the night near Fuji.
- The other option is to drop Amanohashidate and the hop-on hop-off bus to make room.
- The three that matter most for these dates are **Eikando at night**, the **Arashiyama loop** and **Kiyomizu-dera**, because all of them peak in autumn.
